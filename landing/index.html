Being built in public

A digital home for everything your family knows.

Every household accumulates a lifetime of important things — documents, medical records, photographs, recipes, the story of where you came from. Today it lives scattered across drives, chat threads, filing cabinets, and people's memories. Family Knowledge Vault brings all of it into one private, searchable place designed to last generations.

The problem

When you urgently need something, where is it?

A passport before a flight. An insurance policy during a hospital visit. The warranty for an appliance that just failed. Grandma's recipe that nobody wrote down. The information exists — but finding it means searching everywhere at once.

  • Google Drive
  • WhatsApp
  • Email
  • Filing cabinets
  • Phones
  • Laptops
  • People's memories

How it's built

Trust before features.

  • Private by default

    Your family's workspace is yours. Nothing is shared unless you choose to share it, and the most sensitive material is encrypted so that even the server cannot read it.

  • AI that assists, never decides

    Intelligence should reduce the effort of organising and finding — not take over your judgement or replace your memories.

  • Made for everyone at home

    From a grandparent recording a recipe to a teenager reading the family's history — usable without instructions, at any age.

  • Built to last

    This is meant to hold decades of a family's life. It's engineered for preservation, not for a launch.

What works today

  • Create an account and sign in
  • Stay signed in — the session is held in the device keychain
  • Create a family, which owns everything else
  • Invite people with single-use codes that expire, or can be revoked
  • Join a family by entering a code
  • Add relatives who will never sign in — a grandmother, a child
  • Record who is whose parent, spouse or sibling
  • Give people one of four roles, and change it later
  • Remove someone's access, leave a family, or hand it over
  • Delete a family, after typing its name to confirm
  • See what has happened in the family, and who did it
  • File a document — its name, its shelf, whose it is, who can see it, whether AI may read it, and its scans — in one step
  • Sort papers onto six shelves — identity, medical, finance, property, education, legal — and filter by them
  • Attach photographs or PDFs, several to one document, up to 10 MB each
  • Look at an attached photograph without leaving the app
  • Open a PDF in the reader already on your phone
  • Keep a document to yourself, or share it with everyone in the family — and change your mind either way
  • Rename a document, move it to another shelf, or reassign it
  • Archive a document, bring it back, or delete it for good

What does not work yet

  • No password reset. Forget your password today and you are locked out.
  • Email addresses are not verified. Nothing yet proves you own the address you signed up with.
  • Medical records, memories and recipes are empty. The sections exist; the features are Phases 4 to 6. Documents work — they were Phase 3.
  • The "let AI read this" switch does nothing yet. It records your answer and nothing reads it. Asking before there is anything to consent to is deliberate — the alternative is asking a family to agree retroactively — but it is a switch with no machine behind it until Phase 9.
  • You cannot share a document with one person. Sharing is yourself, or the whole family. "Just Mum and Dad" is Phase 10.
  • Nothing is shared between families. A document cannot reach your parents' vault from yours. No mechanism for it has been designed.
  • You cannot search. Six shelves and a filter are the whole of finding something. Search is Phase 8, and reading the text inside a scan is Phase 9.
  • PDFs open in another app. Android's built-in web view cannot draw one, and the usual workaround sends the file to Google to be rendered — which is the opposite of the promise this project makes. So your own reader opens it instead.
  • Deleting a document is permanent. There is no trash, which is why it is offered only after archiving.
  • Guests see no documents at all. Not a filtered list — nothing. The role exists for people who should see the family without its papers.
  • You cannot remove a person from the family. You can take away their access to the app, but a person somebody typed in stays. That is a deliberately separate job, still to come.
  • A deleted family is gone for good. There is no trash and no backup — which is why deleting one makes you type its name first.
  • You can only belong to one family. Nothing lets you switch between your own and your parents'.
  • The family tree does not draw a tree. Relationships are recorded and listed; the picture is Phase 6.
  • End-to-end encryption is designed, not built. Planned for Phase 11, alongside digital legacy.
